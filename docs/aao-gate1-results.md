1 · What Gate 1 is · v1.0

Gate 1 runs entirely outside the org. Read a transcript, put the applicable propositions and the transcript to a model, check the emitted spans, put the surviving claims to a second blind model, and score against a human reading. Nothing is deployed, no objects are created, no sandbox schema is required.

It is deliberately narrow. Extraction, span check, blind re-adjudication, on text. Running the whole stack would make a model failure indistinguishable from a plumbing bug, and the only question worth the cost of a call is whether the model produces correct, citable verdicts.

The bar, ruled 29 July 2026. Zero fabricated, turn-crossing or misattributed spans. Ratification at or above 90%. Zero material misses. Every cell of the sheet filled.

The unacceptable failure mode is a wrong answer confidently marked. Low coverage with high fidelity is explicitly acceptable. This asymmetry is what the whole protocol is built around, and it is why blind re-adjudication gates the write rather than auditing after it.

4 · Run 3 · Teladoc, 7 May · v1.0 — FAILED

Six committed, nine abstained, fifteen propositions. Reviewer ratified six of six. Blind re-adjudication upheld one of six. Blind gates the write, so five claims would never have reached the mirror.

The single survivor, RC_11 (decision-maker access), was established from map rows plus the participant roster. Zero transcript-route claims survived.

The reader was right on all five rejections, and the pattern was one failure repeated: every rejection was a short interpretive bridge that read as comprehension rather than inference.

"we need to prove value" → ROI not documented and agreed
"that's what I've heard" → fact
one key player → the key players as a set
an aversion to administrative burden → the solution fails the most important criterion
an absent field → we do not know it

Span verification could not catch any of these. Every span was real, single-turn and correctly attributed. Fabrication was never the risk on this run. Over-reading was.

The most important result in the programme so far. Human review ratified six of six; the blind reader rejected five of six. On run 2 the two passes agreed almost perfectly. Here they diverged almost completely, and the human pass agreed with every over-reading. Blind re-adjudication caught, cold and on first pass, an error that had got past the extraction, past the extraction's own review, and past the reviewer. This is the empirical case for blind re-adjudication being load-bearing rather than a design preference, and it should be carried to the CTO ahead of any score.

Method defect, mine. The transcript was acquired by DOM scrape of the Lightning page. 62 of 208 turns returned Einstein Conversation Insights marker text — "Longest Monologue", "License Count or Usage Discussion", "Pricing" — or an accessibility label, in place of the utterance. Cause, since diagnosed: the marker renders inside the turn, between the timestamp and the speech, and the parser took the node after the timestamp. Consequence: misses were not gradeable on run 3, and the holed turns were disproportionately the methodology-dense ones. Anything page-derived can substitute interface text for speech invisibly, and span verification cannot catch it because the corruption is upstream of the source of record. The Get Conversation Transcript action returns one field per turn and cannot do this.

Two claims I made on this run and withdraw. That the Lightning page cannot yield the words — it can, via shadow-DOM traversal; what it cannot do is yield them safely. And that a VideoCallRecording row of FileType = TRANSCRIPT is a pointer rather than content — asserted from an empty panel, then falsified by a control which showed the same emptiness on a call that had rendered 208 turns twenty minutes earlier. The condition was session-wide. Recorded here because the reasoning was wrong, not merely the conclusion.

Why the two misses happened, and what replaced the hypothesis

Signature Request, predicted to survive and rejected. The proposition names four elements: an internally approved contract, security documents, the act of submitting, and the purpose of signature. The span — a promise of a future draft SOW — touches none of them. I had reasoned that a missing SOW entails no signature-ready package, but on this deal the MSA had already been sent. This was not a failure of inference distance. It was a part-for-whole substitution: the span addressed one component of a compound proposition.

TC_4, predicted to be rejected and upheld. I treated "there's budget" as one step short of "funds are allocated". It is not a step short; it is a plain assertion of the proposition's content. My verdict was right and my prediction of its fragility was wrong — over-caution in the prediction, not in the verdict.

The rule that replaced it · v1.1

Coextension. A span establishes a verdict when what the span is about is the same thing — at the same scope and the same quantity — as what the proposition is about. Rejection is almost always a scope or quantity mismatch rather than an inference that reached too far.

Every rejection across runs 3 and 4 fits one of three shapes.

Part for whole. Signature Request: an SOW standing for contract plus security documents. Teladoc's Leader Adoption: rows covering the leaders but not their adoption.

Instance for pattern. Path to Partnership: one tentative next step standing for a mutually agreed joint plan.

One for many. Altify Selected and Teladoc's RC_13: a single person's disposition standing for "key players" in the plural. Teladoc's RC_6: hearsay about "the leader" standing for "new sales leaders".

Coextension explains all eight rejections across both runs. Entailment explained five. It is also more actionable, because it can be checked close to mechanically: does the span name every element the proposition names, at the quantity the proposition requires?

The consequence for the charter. Propositions must be decomposed into their named elements, and a verdict requires evidence covering every element. This is the same insight the reviewer reached from the authoring side when he ruled that rubric questions should be written checkably with phased thresholds — one route arrives at decomposing the question, the other at decomposing the evidence, and they meet.

The quantifier gap is now urgent. Three of eight rejections turn on plurality. The corpus contains strict "each" (Emerson's Customer Insights, ruled), bare plurals (Altify Selected, RC_13, RC_6, unruled), and singulars. Only strict-each has a ruled interpretation, so a bare plural is currently a coin flip.

5a · Blind survival rates are not comparable across runs · v1.1

Run 2 upheld seven of eight. Run 3 upheld one of six. Run 4 upheld three of six. These three figures must not be read as a trend, because the runs did not share a single calibrated reader. A stricter reader produces a lower survival rate on identical extraction quality, and nothing in the protocol currently pins reader strictness.

What is comparable, and what the record should rest on, is the reasoning. On runs 3 and 4 every rejection was checked individually and every one was sound. The readers were right; the extraction was loose. But the rate is not yet a metric, and until a fixed reader and a fixed prompt are pinned, it should not be reported as one.

5b · The citation is a set · v1.2 — RULED

A proposition is decomposed into its named elements. A citation is a set of spans covering those elements. A verdict requires coverage of every element.

This follows from coextension rather than being added to it. A proposition naming four things cannot be carried by a span naming one, so the unit of citation has to be able to name four.

Blindness is preserved and this is the point. The reader receives the decomposed proposition and the span set. It does not receive the transcript, the narrative, or the first pass's verdict. The temptation this whole design has to resist is "read the call whole and it's obvious" — that is exactly how a standing Yes comes to sit over zero criteria records. Handing the reader the conversation would convert the only control that has ever caught anything into a second correlated extraction pass.

Byte verification is unaffected. It runs per span, exactly as now, with no new machinery.

Element-wise coverage routes the record state · v1.2

This closes a question left open by an earlier second reader: when blind re-adjudication rejects, where does the claim land? The reader could not answer it, because seeing only one span it could not distinguish this span does not bear on the proposition from nothing in the evidence does. Element-wise coverage answers it structurally.

Coverage	State	Carries
Every named element covered	verdict written	full span set
Some elements covered	UNVERIFIED	the span set that exists
No element covered	not addressed	nothing

All three states already exist in the Glossary, and UNVERIFIED has carried its span since v1.6. No new states are required.

What the rule fails to catch, and it is worth naming · v1.2

Nominal coverage. A set with something touching each element while nothing actually says the thing. Signature Request is the specimen: the MSA had been sent, a security package was offered, an SOW was promised, and signature was never mentioned. Element-wise that reads as nearly covered and it is still wrong, because internally approved and submitted for signature are both unmet.

Set selection is a model judgment. Byte verification proves each span is real. Nothing proves the set is the right set. This is where the residual risk moves, and it should be stated plainly rather than counted as solved.

But the failure modes are asymmetric in the safe direction. Over-cover and the reader should decline to find the proposition established. Under-cover and the result is UNVERIFIED or not-addressed. Only nominal coverage fails unsafely.

Precedent: this is how the methodology already works · v1.2

Multi-record establishment is not new to Altify. Why Act already establishes on Goal ×1 plus Pressure ×5 linked to the mapped decision maker — nobody reads a single insight card to prove a point, they read three to five. A span set is that same logic applied to words rather than cards, so this ruling removes an inconsistency rather than introducing a mechanism.

A note on the asymmetry raised and then withdrawn. It was argued that insight cards arrive pre-ratified item by item because a human wrote each one, whereas a span set is model-selected. The reviewer corrected this: once the system is in play, insight cards will themselves be mostly machine-derived. Human-authored cards become the exception — free text entered against a headless model, routed to a note or an insight by context. So model-selected multiplicity is not a new risk introduced by span sets; it is a risk the card route will already carry. Coextension is therefore the general rule across both evidence kinds.

6 · Rulings harvested · v1.2

These are the durable output of the programme. Scores measure the extraction; these change the design.

The sentence is the proposition. The name is a display label and is never read. Settles a qualifier whose title implied one satisfaction condition and whose sentence implied another, and simultaneously settles the two distinct Stage 4 renewal qualifiers that both display as "Contract Submitted".

Knowledge is a positive indicator even when what is known is bad. An epistemic qualifier is satisfied by knowing, whatever the valence. The badness belongs in the flag, which carries the citation and the reason the content is bad — not in the verdict.

Criteria that surface in conversation are logged as criteria records in loop one, then read as state in loop two. This is the fix for criteria emptiness and it needs no new machinery: the two-loop order already commits the political and insight maps before assessment reads them.

Rubric questions should be authored to be checkable, with stage-phased thresholds — validated with at least one key player early, at least N later — rather than as unfalsifiable universals. Applies directly to Altify's own org, which Altify owns; for customers it becomes guidance rather than a rewrite.

For joint-plan propositions, cite plan-content acts — who does what by when — never meeting logistics. A standing call is not a joint plan.

Establishment may accumulate. Small citations should be able to build toward a verdict across evidence items rather than each item having to carry a proposition alone.

A false on Decision Team should prompt the seller to name at least one holder of each required role, not to achieve coverage on all of them. Flag content, not just flag state.

Coextension governs establishment. Evidence carries a verdict only when it covers every element the proposition names, at the quantity it requires. Proposed v1.1 replacing the falsified entailment hypothesis; ruled v1.2.

The citation unit is a span set, and coverage is adjudicated element by element. Ruled v1.2 — see section 5b.

Plurality is an element carrying a quantity, so a bare plural is decomposed like any other element rather than needing a separate quantifier rule. Raised as an open gap in v1.1; closed v1.2 by decomposition.

Where a proposition's truth condition can be computed from reference data it should be, rather than sought in speech — company size against a defined ICP for strategic-growth questions being the worked example.

7 · Structural defects · v1.2

The sheet offers Agree or Disagree. The reviewer has now needed four states the binary cannot hold.

Agree on different grounds — run 2, Path to Partnership: right verdict, wrong evidence. Conditional agreement — run 3, RC_11: correct if computed state is sufficient, which is itself undecided. Maybe — run 4, Signature Request: genuine uncertainty about overriding a human-written In progress. This was the one row the blind reader went on to reject, and the only one. Added in v1.1. NA — run 4, both criteria rows: not a judgment on the machine at all, but a judgment that the question is malformed and the fix is upstream.

Two of these change what gets written. Two of them are about the rubric rather than the run. Collapsing all four into Disagree destroys the distinction, and collapsing them into Agree inflates ratification. The ratification metric is not currently well-defined, which is why run 4's score is reported as a range.

Correction: how many controls actually fired · v1.2

The architecture is often described as four controls — deterministic verification, blind re-adjudication, ratification, abstention. On the evidence of runs 3 and 4, two of those did work and two did not.

Byte and state verification caught nothing, because nothing was fabricated. Every span across both runs was real, single-turn and correctly attributed. This is not a weakness of the check; it means fabrication was never the failure mode. Over-reading was, and byte comparison is structurally blind to it.

Blind re-adjudication caught five of six on run 3 and three of six on run 4. It is the control that fired.

Ratification caught nothing on run 3 — the reviewer agreed with all six over-readings. On run 4 it produced one hedge that correctly anticipated the single rejection. So ratification functions as a slower calibration loop, not a per-claim gate.

Abstention is a model behaviour, not a control. It cannot catch an error; it is the absence of one.

The accurate description is therefore: deterministic verification plus blind adjudication, with ratification as a calibration loop. That is still a strong architecture and it is the version that should be given to the CTO, because it survives the first hard question.
