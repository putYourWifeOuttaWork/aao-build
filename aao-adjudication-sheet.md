## How this is used · INVERTED at v0.4

**1. The run happens first.** CODE produces one CSV, pre-filled with the model's output, one row per establishment, resolution rows first, every establishment present including the ones he expects to be graded down.

**2. Matthew grades it row by row**, one question at a time, in stage order.

**3. He adds what the read missed** in the blank rows at the bottom. This is now the only channel for UNDERs.

**4. The aggregates compute themselves** from the grade column.

**What the inversion costs, recorded once.** The `model_missed` rate stops being computable as a rate. Misses become a list, not a denominator — a reader who has seen output cannot produce an uncontaminated expectation, and no discipline recovers it. **Findings from an inverted grading state their nature: "three UNDERs found," never "an X% miss rate."** The over-read rate, trap reproductions, and fabrication rate are unaffected; all three are computed against output, which is what exists. The trade was made deliberately, on time: a key that is never written measures nothing at all, and every grading this project has actually completed was inverted.

**Recorded for method, 3 August:** the B&V grading below was done without a key because the run was a projection proof rather than a planned adjudication. Its one UNDER came from Matthew's spontaneous read against his knowledge of the call — which is exactly the channel v0.4 now makes the standing mechanism rather than an accident.

**Grading hazard, recorded 4 August:** the command center truncates spans at the display boundary, and one truncated rendering produced a wrong row in v0.2 (PS7 shown with PO9's span). **Grade against stored spans, never against a display.** *v0.6 added a second specimen — the review CSV truncating at 255 — and it is **withdrawn at v0.7: the stored span was exactly 255 and the CSV agreed.** What the false alarm leaves behind is real and stays: **the byte check cannot catch a shortened quote** — a cut prefix of a real quote is still a substring — so grade-against-stored-spans is the only defence, and it worked here in reverse: checking the stored span is what dissolved the alarm.*

**THE INSTRUMENT'S OWN DEFECTS, new at v1.0, design's, found by a23's grading and named because they changed grades.** The sheet is not neutral and this is the first grading where its gaps are provable.

1. **A row that withholds the criterion's name is not gradeable.** a23's sheet showed `CRITERION`, `REQUIRED`, `FORMAL` in the claimed column, which are the criterion's *properties*, never its identity. Matthew graded deq1 and deq2 FALSE with the note *"NOT ENOUGH CONTEXT TO UNDERSTAND THE CLAIM"* while grading five other criteria rows TRUE on comparable contract-mechanics bytes. **The a23 ruling that a criterion's identity is its name is not only schema law, it is a precondition of grading criteria at all.** Until it is built, criteria rows carry a known instrument defect and their FALSE grades are provisional.
2. **A row that does not mark an internal person invites a wrong grade.** The sheet marked `Wendy Higley [ALTIFY / SELLER]` and Matthew graded it INTERNAL immediately. It did not mark buq2's Pat, whose span says *"who will sign on our side"* spoken by our own seller, and Matthew graded that row TRUE with *"get the person on the damn map."* **Every future sheet marks the internal side on the row, and the marking is derived by the same name-based check the mentioned-person path owes, never by hand.**
3. **Standing, and it follows from both:** the grading sheet is an output of the system and inherits the system's blind spots. A gap in what the sheet can show becomes a gap in what the human can grade, and the human's grade is the only ground truth this project has.

## 2 · Grade vocabulary · fixed enum

| Grade | Meaning | Obligates |
|---|---|---|
| **PASS** | Correct and coextensive. Subject, scope, quantity all match. | Nothing |
| **OVER** | Over-read. Real words stretched past what they carry. **Requires a shape.** | Charter or prompt, design's |
| **UNDER** | Should have established; did not. **Now surfaced only by Matthew's added rows.** | Charter, design's |
| **MIS** | Right statement, wrong person or speaker. | Identity ladder, design's |
| **FAB** | Quote not present in stored text. Should be impossible. | **Defect, CODE's, immediately** |
| **THIN** | Coextensive but too short to read as evidence. | Evidence budget, design's |
| **N/A** | Not gradeable. | Note only |

**OVER shapes:** `part-for-whole`, `instance-for-pattern`, `one-for-many`, `wrong-subject`, `wrong-scope`.

**Matthew's working vocabulary, mapped (ruled at v0.6):** on an upheld row, True = PASS and False = OVER. On a refused row, True = wrongly refused, and **False and Refused are the same grade, rightly refused — no distinction folds.**

**Added at v1.0, from a23:** **INTERNAL** is a grade Matthew uses and the enum did not carry. It is not OVER. An OVER stretches words past what they carry; an INTERNAL row's words carry exactly what they say about a person who is structurally ineligible to be the subject. It obligates the internal-domain gate, never the charter's wording, and it is now excluded from the precision denominator by construction rather than by judgment. **DROPPED** is likewise not a verdict but the absence of one, and a dropped row's grade measures the drop, never the read.
